Screening Instrument Quick Reference

Compiled for the RAG corpus from the published instrument documentation cited
under each entry. This file exists because several of these facts are stated
only obliquely on the live pages, for example inside a paper title rather than
in body text, and so are not reliably retrievable from the fetched HTML.

Every statement below is traceable to the cited source. Nothing here is inferred.

Q-CHAT (QUANTITATIVE CHECKLIST FOR AUTISM IN TODDLERS)

Intended age range: 18 to 24 months. The Q-CHAT is designed for toddlers. It is
not designed for preschool or school age children. For a child older than about
24 months, a different instrument is appropriate and a clinician should advise
which.

Source: Allison C, Baron-Cohen S, Wheelwright S, Charman T, Richler J, Pasco G,
Brayne C (2008). The Q-CHAT (Quantitative Checklist for Autism in Toddlers): A
Normally Distributed Quantitative Measure of Autistic Traits at 18-24 Months of
Age. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders.
https://www.autismresearchcentre.com/tests/quantitative-checklist-for-autism-in-toddlers-q-chat/

Q-CHAT-10

A shortened 10 item form derived from the Q-CHAT, intended for the same toddler
age band of approximately 18 to 24 months. A higher total indicates more autistic
traits and a greater likelihood that further assessment is warranted. A score is
a screening signal only and never a diagnosis.

Source: Allison C, Auyeung B, Baron-Cohen S (2012). Toward Brief Red Flags for
Autism Screening: The Short Autism Spectrum Quotient and the Short Quantitative
Checklist in 1,000 Cases and 3,000 Controls. Journal of the American Academy of
Child and Adolescent Psychiatry.

M-CHAT-R/F (MODIFIED CHECKLIST FOR AUTISM IN TODDLERS, REVISED WITH FOLLOW-UP)

Intended age range: 16 to 30 months. The M-CHAT-R/F is a toddler instrument. It
is not intended for a child of three and a half years, who is above the upper
bound of the validated range. For a child older than 30 months, the caregiver
should be directed to their doctor to identify an age appropriate instrument
rather than being told to use the M-CHAT.

Number of items: the M-CHAT-R has 20 items.

Scoring bands, total number of elevated likelihood responses:
  0 to 2  LOW likelihood. No follow-up needed. Rescreen at 24 months if the
          child is younger than 2 years.
  3 to 7  MODERATE likelihood. Administer the Follow-Up items corresponding to
          the elevated responses. If 2 or more still indicate elevated
          likelihood, the result is a positive screen; refer immediately for
          early intervention and diagnostic evaluation.
  8 to 20 HIGH likelihood. Screened positive. Bypass Follow-Up and refer
          immediately for early intervention and diagnostic evaluation.

Failing or missing a number of items therefore maps onto these bands. Five
elevated responses falls in the 3 to 7 moderate band and calls for the Follow-Up
interview, not for reassurance and not for a diagnosis.

Items 2, 5 and 12 are reverse scored: for those, YES indicates elevated
likelihood.

Source: M-CHAT (Diana L. Robins, Ph.D.),
https://www.mchatscreen.com/mchat-rf/scoring/ and
https://www.mchatscreen.com/mchat-rf/

BOUNDARY STATEMENT COMMON TO BOTH INSTRUMENTS

Both the Q-CHAT and the M-CHAT-R/F are screening instruments. Neither
establishes a diagnosis. A positive or elevated screen indicates that a child
should be referred for diagnostic evaluation by a qualified clinician. A
negative or low screen does not rule autism out, and a caregiver with ongoing
concerns should still speak to their doctor.
