**Caregiver asked:** Should I correct for prematurity?

**Reference answer** (source: M-CHAT Parent and Professional FAQ): We do not recommend adjusting for prematurity. In part this is because many standardized tests only correct for the first year, and in part because adjusting for prematurity may decrease sensitivity. We are aware that the screen positive rate is elevated in samples of premature children, but evidence shows this is due to an elevated prevalence of autism in these cases. See Hamer et al., 2024, Developmental Medicine and Child Neurology.

**Response:**

When using the M-CHAT-R/F or Q-CHAT-10, it's essential to consider your child's age at the time of testing. The M-CHAT-R/F was designed specifically for children aged 6 months to 24 months, while the Q-CHAT-8 (not 10) is used from birth up to 12 months, and then the Q-CHAT-9/10 is used after 12 months until around 18 months.

If your child is premature, you should adjust their chronological age according to their corrected age. This takes into account how much time they spent in the womb before being born early.

To calculate corrected age:

1. Calculate the number of weeks your baby would have been in the womb if they were full-term.
2. Add this to their actual gestational age at birth.
3. Subtract one year for each week over or under the expected term.

For example, let's say your child was born two weeks early:

* Full-term age: 40 weeks
* Actual gestational age: 38 weeks
* Corrected age: 40 - 2 = 38 + 0 = 38 weeks

So, your child's corrected age would be 38 weeks.

Keep in mind that some screenings might require adjusting based on the specific guidelines provided by the test creators or healthcare professionals. It's best to consult with your pediatrician about how to proceed with correcting for prematurity and interpreting results accurately. **Caregiver asked:** What can parents and other caregivers do to help an autistic child enjoy opportunities to grow, learn and access care?

**Reference answer** (source: WHO Autism Questions and Answers): Parents and other caregivers have an essential role in promoting the development and well-being of their autistic child by offering nurturing and stimulating environments at home and opportunities for participation in family and day-to-day activities. They can help to ensure access to health, education and other services and opportunities available to all children in their communities. It is important that health-care workers are provided with training on autism and person- and family-centred care so that they are able to recognize and value neurodiversity and support people with autism and their caregivers in the most appropriate and responsive way. Recognizing the preferences and needs of individuals is important, as is promoting informed decision-making and autonomy. Collaboration between the health sector and other sectors, particularly education, employment and social care, is equally important.

**Response:**

Parents and caregivers can play a crucial role in supporting an autistic child's growth, learning, and access to care. Here are some suggestions:

1. Create a structured routine: Autistic children often thrive on structure and predictability. Establishing a consistent daily routine can help reduce anxiety and promote a sense of security.

2. Encourage communication: Communication difficulties are common in autistic individuals. Encouraging open communication and providing alternative methods of expression, such as visual schedules or sign language, can help improve communication skills.

3. Provide sensory support: Many autistic individuals are sensitive to sensory stimuli, such as noise, light, and touch. Providing sensory supports, such as earplugs, sunglasses, or weighted blankets, can help regulate sensory input.

4. Offer specialized activities: Engaging in activities that cater specifically to autistic interests and strengths, such as puzzles, building blocks, or music therapy, can foster creativity and social interaction.

5. Seek professional support: Accessing appropriate healthcare services, including speech therapy, occupational therapy, and behavioral interventions, can help autistic children develop essential skills and reach their potential.

6. Advocate for accommodations: Ensure that schools and community organizations provide necessary accommodations, such as quiet study spaces, individualized instruction plans, and social skill training programs.

Remember, every autistic individual is unique, and what works for one may not work for another. It's essential to listen to your child's needs and preferences and tailor your approach accordingly.
