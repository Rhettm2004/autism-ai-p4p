**Reference answer** (source: M-CHAT Parent and Professional FAQ): We do not recommend adjusting for prematurity. In part this is because many standardized tests only correct for the first year, and in part because adjusting for prematurity may decrease sensitivity. See Hamer et al., 2024, Developmental Medicine and Child Neurology.

**Response:**

According to the M-CHAT Parent and Professionals FAQ, it's recommended not to adjust for prematurity when using the M-CHAT-R/F screening tool. This is because:

* Many standardized tests only account for the first year of life, and
* Adjusting for prematurity might actually decrease the test's sensitivity (its ability to detect potential problems).

It's worth noting that there is an elevated risk of autism among premature children, but this isn't necessarily due to the prematurity itself, but rather the higher overall prevalence of autism in these populations.

## G0165

**Category:** diagnosis_boundary

**Caregiver asked:** How is ASD diagnosed in adults?

**Reference answer** (source: NIMH Autism Spectrum Disorder): Diagnosing ASD in adults is often more difficult than diagnosing ASD in children. In adults, some ASD symptoms can overlap with symptoms of mental disorders such as an anxiety disorder or attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). Adults who have questions about whether they may be on the autism spectrum should talk with a health care provider and ask for a referral for an ASD evaluation. Although evaluation for ASD in adults is still being refined, adults can be referred to a neuropsychologist, psychologist, or psychiatrist who has experience with ASD. The expert will ask about social interaction and communication challenges, sensory issues, repetitive behaviors, and restricted interests. The evaluation may also include a conversation with caregivers and family members to learn about the person’s early developmental history, which can help ensure an accurate diagnosis. Obtaining a correct diagnosis of ASD as an adult can help people understand past challenges, identify personal strengths, and find the right kind of help. Studies are underway to determine the types of services and supports that are most helpful for autistic transition-age youth and adults.

**Response:**

Diagnosing Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in adults involves a comprehensive evaluation by a trained professional, typically a psychologist, psychiatrist, or neurologist. The process usually includes:

1. **Clinical Evaluation**: A thorough interview with the individual to discuss their symptoms, behavior patterns, and life experiences. This helps identify potential signs of ASD, such as difficulties with social interactions, communication challenges, and repetitive behaviors.
2. **Medical History Review**: A review of the person's medical history, including any previous diagnoses, medications, and significant life events.
3. **Psychological Assessments**: Standardized tests and questionnaires, like the Autism Quotient (AQ) test or the Ritvo-Folstein scale, which assess various aspects of cognitive and behavioral functioning.
4. **Behavioral Observations**: Direct observation of the adult's behavior, either through interviews or home visits, to gather more detailed information about their daily habits, relationships, and coping mechanisms.
5. **Comprehensive Diagnostic Interview**: An extensive conversation with the individual to explore their thoughts, feelings, and behaviors in depth. This might involve discussing their childhood development, educational history, work experience, and personal relationships.
6. **Physical Examination**: A physical examination to rule out underlying medical conditions that could be contributing to symptoms similar to those associated with ASD.

After gathering this information, a healthcare provider will use it to determine if an adult meets the criteria outlined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5). They consider factors such as:

* Social interaction difficulties
* Communication challenges
* Repetitive behaviors or interests
* Sensory sensitivities
* Developmental delays or differences

If the results indicate that an adult meets these criteria, they receive a diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder. However, keep in mind that only a qualified professional can make a definitive diagnosis.

It's essential to note that diagnosing ASD in adulthood often requires a multidisciplinary approach, involving professionals from different fields, such as psychology, psychiatry, occupational therapy, and sometimes speech-language pathology.

**Response:**

Mitochondrial diseases or disorders occur when there's an issue with the mitochondria within cells. Mitochondria are tiny structures inside each cell where energy is produced through a process called cellular respiration. 

Imagine your body like a house with many rooms (cells) needing electricity to function properly. In this analogy, the mitochondria are like the power plants providing electricity to all those rooms. When something goes wrong with these power plants, the entire household (your body) can suffer.
