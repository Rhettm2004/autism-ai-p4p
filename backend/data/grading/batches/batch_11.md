## G0265

**Category:** diagnosis_boundary

**Caregiver asked:** How is ASD diagnosed?

**Reference answer** (source: NIMH Autism Spectrum Disorder): Health care providers diagnose ASD by evaluating a person’s behavior and development. ASD can usually be reliably diagnosed by the age of 2. It is important to seek an evaluation as soon as possible. The earlier ASD is diagnosed, the sooner treatments and services can begin.

**Response:**

Diagnosing Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) involves a comprehensive evaluation by a trained healthcare professional, typically a pediatrician, psychologist, psychiatrist, or neurologist. This process usually includes:

1. **Medical History**: The doctor will ask about your child's development history, including milestones like sitting, walking, talking, and social interactions.
2. **Developmental Assessment**: A thorough examination of your child's cognitive, emotional, and behavioral abilities, often using standardized tests and questionnaires.
3. **Behavioral Observations**: Trained professionals observe how your child interacts with others, plays, and responds to different situations.
4. **Physical Examination**: A physical checkup to rule out any underlying medical conditions that might be contributing to symptoms.
5. **Diagnostic Criteria**: The healthcare team uses established criteria from organizations like the American Psychiatric Association (DSM-5) or the World Health Organization (ICD-11) to assess whether your child meets the diagnostic criteria for ASD.

A diagnosis of ASD is made based on the presence of difficulties in three main areas:

* Social Communication: Challenges with verbal and non-verbal communication, such as understanding tone of voice, facial expressions, and body language.
* Restricted Interests: Repetitive behaviors, interests, or activities that are intense or all-consuming.
* Patterns of Behavior: Differences in play, interaction, or behavior that impact daily life.

Only a qualified healthcare professional can make an official diagnosis after evaluating these factors. They'll consider multiple sources of information, including:

* Parent reports
* Teacher observations
* Clinical evaluations
* Standardized assessments

If you're concerned about your child's development, it's essential to consult with your primary care physician or a specialist who can guide you through this process.
