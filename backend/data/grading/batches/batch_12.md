**Response:**

Diagnosing Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in older children and adolescents involves a comprehensive evaluation by a trained professional, usually a psychologist, psychiatrist, or neurologist. This process typically includes:

1. **Comprehensive Clinical Evaluation**: A thorough interview with the individual, parents, or guardians to gather detailed information about their developmental history, behavior, social interactions, communication patterns, and any challenges they face.

* CARS (Childhood Autism Rating Scale)
* SCQ (Social Communication Questionnaire)

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that all children receive an ASD screening at ages 18 months, 24 months, 30 months, 36 months, 48 months, and 60 months. For those who screen positive, further evaluation should occur between 12 and 18 years old.

**Caregiver asked:** What happens during an additional diagnostic evaluation?

**Reference answer** (source: NIMH Autism Spectrum Disorder): It is important to accurately detect and diagnose children with ASD as early as possible, as this will shed light on their unique strengths and challenges. Early detection can also help caregivers determine which services, educational programs, and behavioral therapies are most likely to be helpful for their child. A team of health care providers who have experience diagnosing ASD will conduct the diagnostic evaluation. This team may include child neurologists, developmental behavioral pediatricians, speech-language pathologists, child psychologists and psychiatrists, educational specialists, and occupational therapists. The diagnostic evaluation is likely to include medical and neurological examinations, assessment of the child’s cognitive abilities, assessment of the child’s speech and language abilities, observation of the child’s behavior, an in-depth conversation with the child’s caregivers about the child’s behavior and development, assessment of age-appropriate skills needed to complete daily activities independently, such as eating, dressing, and toileting, and questions about the child’s family history. Because ASD is a complex disorder that sometimes occurs with other conditions or learning disorders, the comprehensive evaluation may include blood tests and a hearing test. The outcome of this evaluation may result in a formal diagnosis and recommendations for treatment.

**Response:**

During an additional diagnostic evaluation, a team of healthcare professionals with expertise in assessing and diagnosing Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) will work together to gather more information about your child's development, behavior, and overall well-being. 

This team typically consists of experts like child neurologists, Developmental Behavioral Pediatricians, Speech-Language Pathologists, Child Psychologists, Psychiatrists, Educational Specialists, and Occupational Therapists.
