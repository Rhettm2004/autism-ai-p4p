## G0388

**Category:** screening_item_clarification

**Caregiver asked:** What are examples of restricted or repetitive behaviors and interests related to ASD?

**Reference answer** (source: CDC Signs and Symptoms of Autism Spectrum Disorder): People with ASD have behaviors or interests that can seem unusual. These behaviors or interests set ASD apart from conditions defined by problems with social communication and interaction only. Examples of restricted or repetitive behaviors and interests related to ASD can include lines up toys or other objects and gets upset when order is changed; repeats words or phrases over and over (called echolalia); plays with toys the same way every time; is focused on parts of objects (for example, wheels); gets upset by minor changes; has obsessive interests; must follow certain routines; flaps hands, rocks body, or spins self in circles; has unusual reactions to the way things sound, smell, taste, look, or feel.

**Response:**

Restricted or Repetitive Behaviors (RRB) and Interests (RI) are common characteristics associated with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). These might include:

* **Repetitive movements:** Hand flapping, body rocking, pacing back and forth, or repeating actions like tapping hands on surfaces.
* **Insistence on sameness:** Being very particular about routines, rituals, or daily activities being done exactly the same way every time.
* **Strong attachment to specific objects:** Becoming overly focused on collecting certain items, like toys, stamps, or coins, and having trouble letting go of these possessions.
* **Sensory sensitivities:** Being extremely sensitive to sounds, smells, tastes, textures, or lights, which could lead to strong reactions or avoidance behaviors.
* **Specialized knowledge or intense focus:** Developing an extraordinary interest in something, like dinosaurs, music, or video games, and spending excessive amounts of time learning more about it.
* **Rigid thinking patterns:** Struggling with changes in plans or unexpected events, preferring things to be predictable and following strict rules.
* **Self-soothing habits:** Engaging in self-calming strategies, such as hand twiddling, hair pulling, or nail biting.

Keep in mind that everyone exhibits some of these traits from time to time, but people with ASD often display these behaviors more frequently or intensely than others. It's essential to remember that these signs alone do not confirm or rule out ASD; they're just indicators that warrant further evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional.
