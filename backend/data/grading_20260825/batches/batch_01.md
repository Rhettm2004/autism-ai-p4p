**Response:**

Think of it like this: Imagine you're looking at a big jar filled with different colored balls representing children's scores from a screening test. The prevalence would be the percentage of red balls (children scoring high risk) compared to blue balls (those not scoring high risk).

For example, if 10% of children scored above the cut-off on a particular screening tool, that means out of every 100 children tested, 10 are considered "high-risk" based on their results. ## G0026

**Category:** general_autism_knowledge

**Caregiver asked:** What Is Prevalence?

**Reference answer** (source: CDC Data and Statistics on Autism Spectrum Disorder): Prevalence is the number of people in a population that have a condition, relative to all the people in the population. Prevalence is typically shown as a percentage (such as 1%) or a proportion (such as 1 in 100).

**Response:**

Prevalence refers to the total number of cases of a particular condition or disorder within a population at a given time. In other words, it's the overall proportion of people in a group who have that specific condition.

For autism spectrum disorder (ASD), prevalence is often expressed as a percentage of the general population. This can help us understand how common ASD is and identify trends over time.

However, I want to emphasize that knowing the prevalence rate doesn't mean you can determine whether your child has ASD based on this information alone. A diagnosis requires a comprehensive evaluation by a qualified professional.

If you're concerned about your child's development, it's essential to speak with their healthcare provider about screening and further assessment if needed.
