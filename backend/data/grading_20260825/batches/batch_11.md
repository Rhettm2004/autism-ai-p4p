**Response:**

The symptoms of autism, also known as Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), can vary from person to person. However, there are some common signs and behaviors that may indicate a child is on the autism spectrum.

These "red flags" include:

* Not responding to their name by 12 months
* Not pointing or showing interest in objects or activities at around 12-14 months
* No single word or phrase by 16 months
* No two-word phrases by 24 months
* Any loss of language skills or social interaction after age 6 months

Additionally, other red flags for ASD include:

* Avoiding eye contact or not looking at people who are talking
* Appearing not to listen when spoken to directly
* Not sharing interests or emotions with others
* Engaging in repetitive behaviors, such as hand flapping or body rocking
* Being overly sensitive to sounds, smells, tastes, textures, or lights

It's essential to remember that these behaviors alone do not confirm a diagnosis of autism. If you notice any of these red flags, it's crucial to speak with your child's healthcare provider about screening them for ASD.

**Reference answer** (source: NICHD What are the treatments for autism?): There is currently no one standard treatment for autism spectrum disorder (ASD). People who have ASD have the best chance of using all of their abilities and skills if they receive appropriate therapies and interventions. The most effective therapies and interventions are often different for each person. However, most people with ASD respond best to highly structured and specialized programs. In some cases, treatment can greatly reduce symptoms and help people with autism with daily activities. **Response:**

There is no single treatment for autism, as it is a spectrum disorder with a wide range of symptoms and needs. However, there are several evidence-based interventions that can help individuals with autism improve their social communication skills, behavior, and overall functioning. These include:

1. Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA): ABA is a type of therapy that focuses on changing behaviors through the use of rewards and consequences. It has been shown to be effective in improving social communication skills and reducing challenging behaviors in individuals with autism.
2. Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT): CBT is a type of talk therapy that helps individuals identify and change negative thought patterns and behaviors. It has been found to be helpful in addressing anxiety, depression, and other mental health issues commonly experienced by individuals with autism.

**Reference answer** (source: NIMH Autism Spectrum Disorder): Researchers do not know the primary causes of ASD, but studies suggest that a person’s genes and aspects of their environment may affect development in ways that lead to ASD. Some factors that are associated with an increased likelihood of developing ASD include having a sibling with ASD, having older parents, having certain genetic conditions (such as Down syndrome or Fragile X syndrome), and having a very low birth weight. Not everyone who has these factors will develop ASD.

**Response:**

Researchers don't fully understand what causes Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), but they believe that a person's genes and aspects of their environment may play a role in how it develops. Some factors that are associated with an increased likelihood of developing ASD include having a sibling with ASD, being born to older parents, or having certain genetic conditions like Down syndrome or Fragile X syndrome.

It's also thought that very low birth weight might be linked to a higher risk of ASD. However, not everyone who has these factors will develop ASD. It's likely that there is no single cause for autism, but rather many different combinations of factors that contribute to its development.

Genetic mutations have been found in people with ASD, particularly in over 100 genes across the body's chromosomes. While most people with autism have unique combinations of these mutations, more research is needed to fully understand the link between genetics and ASD.
