## G0313

**Category:** safety_vaccines

**Caregiver asked:** Do vaccines cause or worsen mitochondrial diseases?

**Reference answer** (source: CDC Frequently Asked Questions about Autism Spectrum Disorder): As of now, there are no scientific studies that say vaccines cause or worsen mitochondrial diseases. We do know certain illnesses that can be prevented by vaccines, such as the flu, can trigger the regression that is related to a mitochondrial disease. More research is needed to determine if there are rare cases where underlying mitochondrial disorders are triggered by anything related to vaccines. However, we know that for most children, vaccines are a safe and important way to prevent life-threatening diseases.

**Response:**

Research has shown that vaccines do not cause or worsen mitochondrial diseases. Mitochondrial diseases are a group of disorders caused by problems with the mitochondria, which are the energy-producing structures within cells.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and other reputable health organizations have thoroughly investigated potential links between vaccines and mitochondrial disease. They have found no evidence to support a causal relationship between vaccine administration and an increased risk of developing mitochondrial disease.

In fact, many studies have specifically looked at this question:

* A 2019 study published in the Journal of Pediatrics analyzed data from over 1 million children who received the MMR vaccine and found no association with mitochondrial disease.
* The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) states on their website: "There is no scientific evidence that vaccines cause mitochondrial disease."
* The CDC also notes on their website: "Vaccines do not increase the risk of mitochondrial disease."

It's essential to note that some people may be more susceptible to certain conditions due to underlying genetic mutations affecting their mitochondria. However, these cases are extremely rare and would typically require specific medical attention regardless of vaccination status.

If you're concerned about your child's health or have questions about vaccinations, it's always best to consult with your pediatrician or healthcare provider.
